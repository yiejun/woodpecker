Termites eat from the inside out

Termites avoid light and tunnel deep inside wooden structures. They leave the outer surface intact. By the time visible damage appears, the wood may have already lost much of its structural strength.

In New Orleans, termite damage and prevention are estimated to cost approximately $300 million annually. Centuries-old wooden temples and palaces across East Asia face similar risks. Early detection is crucial to preserve cultural heritage.

Hollow wood sounds different.

Woodpeckers tap on trees and use sound to locate hollow spaces beneath the surface. Our robot applies the same principle. Solid wood produces a short, sharp sound, but hollow wood produces a deeper, longer-lasting resonance. Listen to the difference yourself.

From one tap to a diagnosis

  1. Strike

    The striker hits the surface with the same force at regular intervals. Only when the force is constant does a difference in sound mean a difference in the wood.

  2. Capture

    A piezoelectric sensor placed against the wood detects vibrations traveling through the timber, which minimizes interference from surrounding noise.

  3. Feature extraction

    The signal is segmented at the moment of impact, normalized, and transformed into a time-frequency representation to analyze how vibrations change over time.

  4. Classification

    A one-dimensional convolutional neural network analyzes vibration decay rates and resonance patterns to distinguish solid wood from wood with potential internal cavities.

  5. Damage map

    Results from each tapping location are combined to create a damage map of the entire wooden post to track changes and compare with future inspections.
